Mortgages and Financing in Mexico for Foreign Buyers

Benjamin Brasher
Updated 23 November 2026 · 2 min read
In this article
  1. Mexican bank mortgages
  2. Developer financing
  3. Seller financing on resales
  4. HELOC in the USA & Canada
  5. Questions

Financing a Mexican property as a foreign buyer is possible, but more limited than at home. The smartest capital is often the equity you already hold.

Mexican bank mortgages

  • Interest rates: historically around 9 to 12% a year, in Mexican pesos.
  • Down payment: most banks require 30 to 40% from foreign applicants.
  • Documentation: income verification, credit history, valid visa or residency, and an RFC (Mexican tax ID).

Developer financing

Many developers offer in-house, USD-denominated payment plans for pre-construction, typically 30 to 50% down, with the balance paid in instalments through construction. Read every term carefully.

Seller financing on resales

Rare, but worth knowing. In a buyer's market like today's, it never hurts to ask a seller whether they would finance a portion of the price. For example, a substantial down payment with the balance paid over an agreed term, secured through the notary.

The key is a structured, solid offer: a clear schedule, interest terms and guarantees, drafted by your attorney. Presented well, it can unlock a deal, and better terms, that a bank never would.

HELOC in the USA & Canada

For North American owners, one of the most efficient routes is a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) secured against a primary residence in the United States or Canada. You borrow against equity you already own, in your home currency, and purchase in Mexico as a cash buyer. Terms and tax treatment differ between the U.S. and Canada, so speak with your lender and tax advisor first.

  • Lower cost: HELOC rates are tied to prime and usually sit well below peso mortgage rates.
  • Cash-buyer leverage: stronger negotiating position and a faster, simpler closing.
  • Flexibility: draw only what you need, in USD or CAD, with no peso loan exposure.
  • Canadian buyers: HELOCs from the major banks (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC) are widely used for exactly this purpose.

Questions

Can foreigners get a mortgage in Mexico?

Yes, but terms are tighter than at home: interest rates have historically been around 9 to 12% a year in pesos, and most banks require 30 to 40% down from foreign applicants.

What down payment do developers ask for pre-construction?

Typically 30 to 50% down, with the balance paid in instalments through construction.
